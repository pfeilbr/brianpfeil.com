# The people who held me together

My life with bipolar

===

## This one is different

I've been dealing with what my doctors diagnosed as bipolar disorder. I've kept most of it private for years, and the privacy cost me more than it protected me.

So I'm writing it down: partly for the people I've hurt, partly to thank the people who held me together anyway, and mostly for anyone reading this at 2 a.m. who recognizes themselves in it.

===

## Where it starts: my father

My father had schizophrenia, and was diagnosed with schizoaffective disorder: schizophrenia together with the highs and the depressions. I grew up with it in the house. I knew the weather could change without warning.

He was involuntarily committed many times. Each one was a crisis for him and for all of us.

What I didn't expect was to catch myself doing the things I remembered him doing.

===

## My mom

My personality is hers. I learned what unconditional love is from her: first by receiving it, then by watching her care for my father for as long as she possibly could.

She taught me to garden and to cross-stitch. We had tea together. I licked the batter off the spoon.

She's living with dementia now. Whatever she forgets, what she taught me stays.

===

## Looking back: the obsessions

When something caught me, it caught me completely. Ski movies with the sound off, to study every turn. Every component of every mountain bike. A twelve-pack of LaCroix a day.

I don't know how much of that is the illness and how much is simply who I am. But I recognize the same all-in intensity in my highs.

===

## The part that feels good

Nobody warns you that the high is the part you'll miss.

I needed very little sleep and didn't feel tired. I had ideas faster than I could write them down.

- The gym twice a day.
- Clubs and dancing, late nights, loud music.
- A flood of new friends.

From the inside it doesn't feel like an illness. That's exactly what makes it dangerous.

===

## The part that doesn't

I got **aggressive** with people. People who loved me started to feel like obstacles.

I **lied**. A steady stream of small untruths to cover where I'd been, what I'd spent, how late it was.

Then the crash. Depression is an absence. I went quiet for months. From the inside, answering a text feels like lifting a car.

===

## The people around me

**My ex-wife** spent years as part of a team trying to get me well. She carried more of it than anyone should have to.

**My boys** saw both versions of their dad. My older son researched all of this on his own and became my best friend. My younger son, my skiing buddy, lived through the worst of it.

I work very hard for my story to end differently for them.

===

## Doc

We met on a date at a coffee shop and agreed we were better as friends. One of the best decisions either of us made.

Hikes. Scrabble. A playlist about love and heartbreak that I still play. She was in school, working nights and raising two daughters, and she made time for me anyway.

Doc, thank you.

===

## L.

My first relationship after my divorce, and it was wonderful. She shared her culture and her cooking with me, and she learned to mountain bike, fish and kayak because they mattered to me.

Her daughters were so kind to me, and they didn't have to be.

This was one of the good things, and it was real. L., thank you.

===

## Se Jin

Good morning and good night texts, every day, for six months. Then the depression came, and I ghosted her.

About a month ago we found each other again, and she picked it right back up. No hard feelings. Nothing.

That kind of grace is rarer than it should be. I'm so thankful to have her in my life again, and Gracie too.

===

## E.

E. trusted me, when trust was exactly what I'd been burning through.

She let me into her life, and her son and I had so much fun together.

She wanted to get me on the right track: no more drinking, no more partying. She cared where I was headed, and she helped me get there.

===

## Jason and Michelle

If this story has a hero, it's my brother Jason.

I texted him every day with what I'd done, and he read every one. He helped with my finances and my divorce. For Dad, for Mom, for all of us, he figures it out. His wife Michelle is right beside him.

He kept supporting me even after I lied to him. Thank you, both of you.

===

## Getting help

In the fall of 2022 I had an episode that was completely out of character, and I recognized it.

I emailed a long list of therapists and told them plainly: *My first goal is an assessment. I need to know if I am in fact bipolar.*

I was diagnosed in December 2022.

===

## Trial, error, and patience

An SSRI. Then Vraylar. Then Prozac. Ketamine infusions. TMS.

None of these was a magic switch. Every change meant weeks of waiting.

The most important thing I've learned: **"this medication didn't work" is information, not failure.** Keep going back.

===

## Work and brain fog

Depression takes away the thing my career is built on: holding a whole system in my head. It's like thinking through wet cement.

I've taken three medical leaves of absence.

**Taking leave is treatment. It is not quitting.**

===

## The label

In late 2024 my psychiatrist and psychologist told me they no longer believed the bipolar diagnosis was correct.

Diagnosis in psychiatry is hard. If yours has changed, you are not a fraud. The experiences were real, whatever the name.

I've learned to **hold the label loosely and the treatment tightly.**

===

## What I wish someone had told me

- **Tell your doctor about the highs**, not just the lows.
- **Track your mood**, sleep and energy daily.
- **Protect sleep** like it's medication.
- **Build a team**, and let them in. Find your Jason.
- **Repair where you can**, without excuses.
- **Be patient with treatment.**

===

## If you're in it right now

- **Call or text 988**: Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (U.S.), any time
- **Text HOME to 741741**: Crisis Text Line
- **1-800-950-6264**: NAMI HelpLine
- **dbsalliance.org**: free peer support groups
- Outside the U.S.: **findahelpline.com**

In immediate danger? Call 911 or go to the nearest ER.

===

## No neat ending

I'm still in treatment, still learning my patterns, still repairing relationships. But I'm here, I'm present for my boys, and I'm done hiding this.

If you're going through something similar, message me. Consider this me going first.

*I'm not a medical professional. This is my experience, not medical advice.*
