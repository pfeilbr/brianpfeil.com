# The highs, the lows, and everything in between

My life with bipolar

===

## This one is different

I've been dealing with what my doctors diagnosed as bipolar disorder. I've kept most of it private for years, and the privacy cost me more than it protected me.

So I'm writing it down: partly for me, partly for the people I've hurt along the way, and mostly for anyone reading this at 2 a.m. who recognizes themselves in it.

===

## Where it starts: my father

My father had bipolar disorder. I grew up with it in the house. I didn't have words for it as a kid, but I knew the weather could change without warning.

Later I watched him decline, in and out of hospitals, less and less himself.

What I didn't expect was to catch myself doing the things I remembered him doing.

===

## The part that feels good

Nobody warns you that the high is the part you'll miss.

I needed very little sleep and didn't feel tired. I had ideas faster than I could write them down, and every one felt obviously right.

- The gym twice a day.
- Clubs and dancing, late nights, loud music.
- A flood of new friends.

From the inside it doesn't feel like an illness. That's exactly what makes it dangerous.

===

## The part that doesn't

I got **aggressive** with people: short-tempered, sure I was right. People who loved me started to feel like obstacles.

I **lied**. A steady stream of small untruths to cover where I'd been, what I'd spent, how late it was.

Then the crash. Depression is an absence. The friends I collected so fast drifted away just as fast. From the inside, answering a text feels like lifting a car.

===

## The people around me

**My ex-wife** spent years as part of a team trying to get me well. She carried more of it than anyone should have to.

**My boys** saw the too-intense version of their dad, and the version that couldn't get off the couch. I work very hard for my story to end differently for them.

**Friends** came and went with my moods. If you're one of them: it wasn't you.

===

## Getting help

In the fall of 2022 I had an episode that was completely out of character, and I recognized it. It looked exactly like what I'd seen growing up with my father.

I emailed a long list of therapists and told them plainly: *My first goal is an assessment. I need to know if I am in fact bipolar.*

I was diagnosed in December 2022.

===

## Trial, error, and patience

An SSRI. Then Vraylar. Then Prozac. Ketamine infusions. TMS.

None of these was a magic switch. Every change meant weeks of waiting to see whether it was working.

The most important thing I've learned: **"this medication didn't work" is information, not failure.** It narrows the search. Keep going back.

===

## Work and brain fog

Depression takes away the thing my identity is built on: holding a whole system in my head. It's like thinking through wet cement.

I've taken three medical leaves of absence. Writing the email that said "I've been dealing with severe depression" was one of the scariest things I've done professionally.

**Taking leave is treatment. It is not quitting.**

===

## The label

In late 2024 my psychiatrist and psychologist told me they no longer believed the bipolar diagnosis was correct. Grief and trauma may explain much of it.

Diagnosis in psychiatry is hard. If yours has changed, you are not a fraud.

The experiences were real, whatever the name. I've learned to **hold the label loosely and the treatment tightly.**

===

## What I wish someone had told me

- **Tell your doctor about the highs**, not just the lows.
- **Track your mood**, sleep and energy daily.
- **Protect sleep** like it's medication.
- **Build a team**, and let them in.
- **Repair where you can**, without excuses.
- **Be patient with treatment.** The second or third try is normal.

===

## If you're in it right now

- **Call or text 988**: Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (U.S.), any time
- **Text HOME to 741741**: Crisis Text Line
- **1-800-950-6264**: NAMI HelpLine
- **dbsalliance.org**: free peer support groups
- Outside the U.S.: **findahelpline.com**

In immediate danger? Call 911 or go to the nearest ER.

===

## No neat ending

I'm still in treatment, still learning my patterns, still repairing relationships. But I'm here, I'm present for my boys, and I'm done hiding this.

If you're going through something similar and just want someone to hear it, message me. Consider this me going first.

*I'm not a medical professional. This is my experience, not medical advice.*
