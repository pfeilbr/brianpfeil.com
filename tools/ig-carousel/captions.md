## condensed

I've kept this private for years. The privacy cost me more than it protected me.

I grew up with my father's schizophrenia in the house. In 2022 I caught myself doing the things I remembered him doing, and that December I was diagnosed with bipolar disorder. Since then: the highs I miss, the crashes, the people I hurt and lost, a long run of medications, ketamine, TMS, three medical leaves from work, and doctors who now question the label itself.

But this is also a thank-you. To my mom, my boys, Doc, L., Se Jin, E., and my brother Jason and his wife Michelle: you held me together when I couldn't do it myself.

I'm sharing it for anyone reading this at 2 a.m. who recognizes themselves in it. If you're struggling, find your Jason. If you already have one, tell them what they mean to you.

If you're going through something similar and just want someone to hear it, DM me. Consider this me going first.

Part 2 of 4: The highs, the lows, and the people around me.

## part-4

Part 4 of 4: Getting help, and the honest version of what came next.

A diagnosis in December 2022. Then a long run of medications, ketamine and TMS, and what I learned: "this medication didn't work" is information, not failure.

Brain fog, and three medical leaves from work. Taking leave is treatment, not quitting. In the U.S., FMLA gives up to 12 weeks of job-protected leave for a serious health condition.

And the complicated part: in late 2024 my doctors told me they no longer believed the bipolar diagnosis was correct. Why I'm sharing this anyway.
